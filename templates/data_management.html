Hosting, providers and locations

This declaration separates observed application behaviour from unconfirmed live infrastructure. The local review uses synthetic records in a disposable local database; this is not evidence of how production is hosted.

Earlier protected catalog inspection identified a Supabase PostgreSQL database for the existing system. A fresh protected provider/location check was unavailable. The deployed web host, database processing region, backup/support locations, provider contracts and subprocessors have not been confirmed. No country or region is asserted for live processing.

Cross-border processing cannot be ruled in or out from the available evidence. The practice must confirm all hosting, support, backup, recipient and operator locations and assess applicable transfer requirements before approving this declaration. An address on a business card is not a data-hosting location.

Access and security controls

The reviewed Flask application uses authenticated accounts, active institution membership, patient assignments and separate clinical grants. Routes, queries, sensitive views and exports apply server-side checks. Assistants and account administrators do not automatically gain diagnosis or note access. Patients receive only their permitted patient-facing information.

The code includes CSRF protection, escaped templates, restricted public assets, no-store dynamic/authenticated responses, generic errors and limited diagnostic logging. Production configuration requires HTTPS handling, secure cookies, trusted hosts and an encrypted database connection setting. Actual production TLS, database-role privileges, backup encryption, key handling and edge behaviour still require confirmation. These controls do not establish end-to-end encryption or guarantee confidentiality.

Signed session cookies hold limited authentication/session/CSRF state and generic UI messages, not clinical text. The current authentication limits are 30 minutes idle and two hours total; session expiry is not deletion of patient records.

Retention, deletion and secure disposal

No approved practice retention schedule or automatic clinical-record disposal process was found in the reviewed implementation. Clinical/care-plan versions, consent decisions, task histories and audit records are preserved. Closing a follow-up, expiring a proposed time or cancelling an appointment is not secure erasure.

Requests to correct, restrict or delete information require an authorised review. Some records may have to be retained under applicable professional/legal duties, a dispute or another justified requirement. A retention decision needs the record category, applicable requirement, authorised owner and reason; no fixed practice retention period is invented here.

Secure disposal for databases, paper records, exported copies, storage devices and backups remains to be approved and evidenced. This declaration does not promise immediate or irreversible deletion on request.

Backups and recovery

Production backup frequency, access, location, retention, encryption, recovery objectives and restore-test results have not been supplied or verified. Local synthetic migration/concurrency tests are not production backup or disaster-recovery tests. The practice must confirm how restored data will respect later restrictions/corrections and how retained backup copies are eventually disposed of.

Audit records and incidents

General application audit events record actor, institution, action, resource identifier and timestamp for sensitive views and changes without copying clinical text into those events. Clinical workflow/version histories may contain clinical information as part of protected records. Production proxy, server, database and monitoring logs need a separate access, minimisation and retention review.

An approved incident handler, reporting channel, response plan and notification procedure have not been provided. There is no automated regulatory filing or incident-response deadline promise in this implementation. The practice must assess and act on applicable notification duties; the Information Regulator’s security-compromise guidance explains its current process. General booking/contact channels are not continuously monitored incident-reporting services.

Calendar, communications, analytics and AI

Calendar and care coordination
Appointments, change proposals, assigned tasks, referrals and review dates are handled internally. No external calendar synchronisation or referral-document transfer is implemented here.
Email and SMS
This checkout uses synthetic delivery payloads and sandbox notification previews. Durable in-app reminders can be queued under approved rules and recorded preferences. No live external email/SMS adapter is connected by this change. The production worker/provider status must be verified separately; a mailbox printed on the card is not proof of an application email integration.
Payments
Earlier schema evidence includes financial records in the wider system. This checkout does not call a live payment gateway. Actual billing recipients, operators and financial retention must be confirmed separately from clinical-record handling.
Analytics and browser services
No analytics tracker, external font service or advertising library is loaded by the reviewed templates. Provider/edge telemetry remains unconfirmed. Essential session and CSRF cookies support the site; no optional marketing consent is bundled into them.
AI and automation
The example chat widget is not included and no external AI service receives patient messages from this implementation. It does not automate diagnosis, medication recommendations, risk probabilities or treatment decisions.
Official links
HPCSA and Information Regulator resources are ordinary external links, not embedded services. Following one leaves this site; no patient or registration details are appended to those URLs.

Monitoring and contact hours

No practice-wide telephone, privacy-inbox or response hours have been confirmed. Optional check-ins display the monitoring hours recorded in the individual agreed plan; they are not continuously monitored. Appointment requests do not promise a response time or emergency support.
